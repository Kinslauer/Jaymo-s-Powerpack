# Kingdoms 0.3: builders, storehouses, theft, barbarians and war

Kingdoms today (0.2, `kingdoms/`): kingdoms founded at village bells, with three kinds of seat built from the village's
own materials (`Palette`), kings (bosses), guards, archers and watchtowers, two-layer reputation, ranks, a treasury
(emeralds), a fallen king's three fates (successor, vassal, ruin) and `/kingdom` commands. 0.2 hasn't been seen in game.

This patch adds everything below. All of it is **original code and art**. Tale of Kingdoms (GPL-3.0, art all rights
reserved) and Thief (GPL-3.0) are only inspiration: nothing is copied from either.

## User's answers
1. **Builders** for every civilization: barbarians, villages, fiefdoms and kingdoms. They dress like their faction and
   grow their settlement into a bigger city while it has resources, people and peace. Buildings go up **in real time**:
   workers walk back and forth between storage and the site.
2. **Barbarians are built now too.**
3. **Resources are real items** in storehouses or sheds. **Theft** works like the Thief mod: stealing has consequences.
4. **Wars are built now too.**
5. **Player builds:** builders never touch player-built land, except on **plots a member lord marks** for them.

## A. Factions (the foundation)
Every settlement is a **faction** with a type:

| Type | What it is | Leader | Folk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kingdom** | as today | king (boss) | guards, archers, builders |
| **Fiefdom** | a kingdom that's someone's vassal (today's "vassal" fate, and land won in war) | lord (boss, a little weaker than a king) | guards, archers, builders, styled in its overlord's colours |
| **Village** | any village bell that isn't a capital (found the same way capitals are) | elder (not a boss: a villager) | vanilla villagers, a village builder crew; the iron golem is its guard |
| **Barbarian camp** | see D | chief (mini-boss) | barbarians, barbarian builders |

- `Kingdom` becomes the general `Faction` (type, name, colour, banner, seat, leader, land, treasury, storehouses, folk
  roster, relations), with today's kingdoms as the Kingdom type. Existing saves are migrated.
- **Relations** between factions: allied, neutral, rival or at war; vassal to overlord. Reputation per player per
  faction as today.
- `/kingdom info` and friends work for every type. A new `/faction` alias covers villages and camps.

## B. Storehouses, the economy and theft
**Storehouses:**
- Every faction has storage made of **real containers**. Kingdoms and fiefdoms have a **storehouse** building by the
  seat. Villages have a **shed**. Camps have a **loot pile** (barrels and chests under a lean-to).
- Each faction builds one when founded (or upgrades the old one), and registers its containers.

**Production:** each in-game day, a faction's people produce **real items** into its storage, scaled by:
- villagers and folk
- farms
- land: forests give logs, stone gives cobble and stone, plus food
- for kingdoms: taxes in emeralds into the treasury

Barbarians produce less but **loot** what their raids carry home. Every number is a constant.

**Economy health:**
- *healthy economy*: enough of each building material in storage for the next building, and food in stock
- *adequate personnel*: enough folk for the settlement's size; dead guards and builders are replaced slowly from the
  population
- *at peace*: not at war (E), not under attack right now

`/kingdom info` shows all three.

**Players and storage:**
- Members with rank (knight and up) may take from their own faction's storage. Lords may also mark plots (C).
- **Donating** (putting items in) raises reputation and standing.

**Theft** (our own system, in the spirit of Thief):
- **What counts as a crime:**
  - Opening or taking from a faction's storage when you're not allowed.
  - Breaking its job blocks, beds, chests or its bell, or killing its livestock or villagers, inside its land.
  - Each crime is **light**, **medium** or **heavy**.
- **Witnesses:** a crime only counts if one of the faction's folk or villagers **sees** it (line of sight within range).
- **What happens when seen:**
  - It lowers your reputation with that faction by its severity.
  - Medium and heavy crimes bring guards (or golems, or barbarians) after you.
  - Heavy ones mark you as an outlaw there for a while.
  - Witnesses shout a line ("Thief!").
- **Unseen theft:** a missing item is noticed later, and that only costs standing with the faction as a whole, not with
  you.

## C. Builders and growing cities
**Builder crews:**
- A new folk role, **builder**: a **foreman** plus workers. A crew is 2–4 to start, growing with the settlement.
- Each faction type dresses them in its style:
  - kingdom: tabards in its colour
  - fiefdom: its overlord's colours
  - village: villager-style work clothes with aprons
  - barbarian: furs and hide
- Skins are generated by `tools/gen_folk.py`. Builders carry what they're hauling visibly.

**The planner:**
- While the economy is healthy, personnel are adequate and the faction is at peace, the foreman picks the next project.
- Each settlement has a growth order, with buildings drawn in code from the faction's `Palette`, like the castles:
  - **Villages:** houses, farms, a well, a market stall, paths, then a wall.
  - **Kingdoms and fiefdoms:** houses, workshops (smithy, mason, stable), farms, a market, barracks, roads, stone
    walls with gates and towers. The city grows outward ring by ring.
  - **Camps:** see D.
- **Where it builds:** sites are picked on open natural ground inside the faction's land, next to roads, never on
  player-placed blocks.
- **Plots:** a lord of the faction can mark one with `/kingdom plot mark` while standing in it, or with a plot stake
  item. Builders may then clear and build there, even over that lord's own builds, and the lord may choose what goes
  on it.

**Building in real time:**
- The foreman stakes out the site (fences, a sign).
- Workers walk to the storage, take a load of the right blocks out of the real containers, carry it to the site and
  place it block by block, bottom up, then go back for more.
- It pauses if materials run out, if it's attacked, or if war starts. It resumes when they're back.
- Half-built buildings stay half-built, are saved, and are finished later.
- **Speed:** a few blocks per worker trip. A house takes about a Minecraft day; a wall section, several days.

**Fallback:** if a worker can't path to the site, they're teleported there with a puff, so it never stalls.

## D. Barbarians
**Camps:**
- They spawn in the wild **150–300 blocks** from kingdoms: wool tents, a log palisade, a campfire, and the chief's tent
  with loot.
- The chief is a **mini-boss** with a boss bar.

**Barbarians:**
- Hostile to players, kingdom folk, villagers and golems.
- Their alerts only call their own side: barbarians help barbarians, and kingdom folk help kingdom folk.

**Growth:** camps grow daily. Their builders add tents, a bigger palisade, watch posts, a totem and pens, from what
their raids loot.

**War parties:**
- A camp raids the nearest kingdom or village when a player is near it.
- The raid is announced, and the party marches on the seat.
- Loot it carries home fills the loot pile.

**Clearing a camp** (killing the chief) earns reputation with every kingdom and village. The camp scatters and rots
away.

## E. War
**Declaring:**
- A kingdom (its NPC king, by relations and rivalry, or a member lord with `/kingdom war declare <name>`) declares war
  on another kingdom or fiefdom.
- A warm-up (one in-game day) is announced to everyone, then the war starts.

**During a war:**
- Construction stops on both sides.
- NPC war parties march on the enemy's land.
- Guards fight on sight.
- Planting your kingdom's banner in the enemy's land and **holding** it (no enemy folk within range for a while)
  **claims that chunk**. Only chunks next to land you already hold can be claimed.

**War score:** from kills (folk, the leader), chunks taken, and storehouse raids.

**Peace:**
- At a war-score threshold or after a set time, peace comes on terms: tribute in real items and emeralds, chunks
  ceded, or **vassalage**, which turns the loser into a fiefdom.
- Losing its seat or its leader can end a war early.

**Reputation:**
- Players who fight in a war gain reputation with the side they help and lose it with the other. Their allies react.
- NPC kingdoms fight each other on their own, as the original plan said.
