# Medieval patch 10: Lost peoples and the mineshafts

- **Old cities**: ruined cities of the four lost peoples now stand in the world. The Sunstep folk built in the deserts, the Pillared folk on the plains and meadows, the Mound-kin in the taiga and snowy plains of the north, and the Walled Court in the cherry groves of Blossom's lands. The cities keep away from villages and never build over anyone else's buildings. Each city has broken streets, worn houses and a great monument: a stepped pyramid, a columned hall, a barrow ringed with standing stones, or a palace. Each also has a temple with an Old Altar (`oldage/OldRuins`, `oldage/People`, `tools/gen_old_age.py`).
- **Tombs and their rulers**: under each monument a stair leads down to a Tomb Seal and a burial chamber with a sarcophagus and grave goods. The people's Old Ruler sleeps there among its Tomb Guards: The Sun-Crowned Dead, The Last Consul, The Barrow-King or The Jade Empress. Breaking the seal or striking a sleeper wakes them all. Each ruler fights with a boss bar, its own theme and its people's moves: a sun curse, a shield bash with a rally, a grave chill or a blink and cut. At half health it calls guards. The guards take hits on their shields from the front (`oldage/OldRuler`, `oldage/TombGuard`, `client/oldage/OldAgeClient`).
- **Old age loot**: tomb and house chests hold each people's relic (Sun Scarab, Marble Bust, Barrow Brooch, Jade Seal), emeralds and, with Jaymo's Powers, the old peoples' arms. The old age's money was emeralds, so its people trade in nothing else. In rich towns the money-changer pays a little more for emeralds, relics and old arms (`oldage/OldAge`, `Currencies`, `Spoils`, `Relics`).
- **Patron champions**: offering a lit Old Altar its patron's gift calls that patron's champion to fight you. Beating it blesses everyone who struck it. The altar goes out and stays dark until it rekindles on its own (`oldage/OldAltarBlock`, `oldage/OldTemples`, `Blessings`).
- **Fight music for every boss**: each patron's champion now has its own battle theme, and so do the brood queen, the Snow Widow, the Unburied, the Great Necromancer, the fortress knight, the kingdom rulers, the horde's grave-captain and every other boss with a bar (`music/FoeThemes`, `tools/gen_foe_themes.py`).
- **Ruin folk**: the first time someone walks into a city, a few of its people's descendants turn up in the huts as Ruin Keepers. They speak the old tongue and sell relics, old arms, trim stones and maps to another people's city, all for emeralds. Their relics and arms always cost more than a changer will pay for them. They buy bread, iron and wool (`oldage/RuinFolk`, `Professions`, `Languages`, `McaCompat`).
- **Miners in the mineshafts**: a settlement near a mineshaft sends two miners into it. They hang lanterns, push the minecarts and dig out ore they can see, but never where water or lava would break in. When their arms are full, or night falls, they carry the ore to the settlement's storehouse and come back. Where the shaft has no way up they can find, the ore goes up without them (`mineshafts/ShaftMiners`, `mineshafts/Mineshafts`).
- **Cave-ins**: an earthquake brings down mineshaft roofs near players. The rock right by a timber frame holds, but the stretches between frames can fall. Miners caught under a fall are shut in until they're dug out (`mineshafts/ShaftCollapse`, `QuakeRescue`, `disasters/Earthquakes`, `disasters/BlockBudget`).
- **Shaft dwellers**: deep mineshafts fill with goblin scouts and diggers, dead miners with picks and caps, and spiders. What comes depends on the depth and the biome. The parts the miners work are mostly kept clear (`mineshafts/ShaftDwellers`).
- **Better mineshaft loot**: with the mineshafts mod (`mmr`) loaded, its chests and barrels also hold copper bits, realm silver, crushed ores, smithing tools, pattern scrolls and relics (`mineshafts/ShaftLoot`).
