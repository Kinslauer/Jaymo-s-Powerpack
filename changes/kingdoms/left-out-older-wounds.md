# Left out of older PRs: companions bury the dead
- **Companions bury you, grieve and remember** (Andy: "if you have companions they should bury you and grieve and bring you up if asked about their previous adventures"). When a player's life ends for good (Powers' final death), every MCA villager within 64 blocks they were involved with (family, hired help, a trusted friend) is a companion. The nearest walks to the body and says the words; the others gather and each grieves in their own line; then the body is taken up (with Kitchen, the player's corpse), what it carried goes into a chest under the grave, and a gravestone goes over it with a sign: "Here lies", the name, what took them, and the day. Every companion remembers (`Mourning.MOURNS`), and MCA's menu offers **Ask about their old adventures** on one who does: they speak of the one they buried, to anyone, the dead one's next life too.
- Villagers no longer try to tend a downed body someone is carrying (`PowersCompat.isCarried`).
- Last Stand never saves a player who gave up where they lay.
- **Folk react to a Smog user, and drive them out** (Andy: "they should react, and repeat interactions should drive you out of the town. most people will come to realize the effects from corruption and the bad karma you must harbor to have gotten there"). A villager who sees you while the smog holds you remarks on it, at most once a minute a town, and thinks a heart less of you. The first two wonder at the ash and the cough; after that they know it for corruption and say what it says of you, and the fourth time you're warned. The fifth time the town drives you out for 10 minutes: a realm with a ruler has its guards see you out; a town without one shouts you off, and if you're still there after 30 s its nearest guard comes at you. A town forgets one remark every in-game day (`SmogFolk`).
- **Slipfin slides out of an Aether Giant's grab** (Andy: "fix it"): its hand closes and the slick alien squirts out between the fingers, even if they turn Slipfin while held.
- **Creative players switch class from a Class tab on the H menu** (docs/Classes-Plan.md promised it): the class screen opens with a class already chosen, and Choose swaps it.
- **Kingdoms keep court casters, and they march** (Andy: "they should be, they should have at least two if they do send one so that they are never without. richer kingdoms will have more"). A kingdom now keeps two court casters (wizards, or necromancers under a tyrant), three once it's prosperous or its treasury holds 300, four once it's wealthy. When a war party musters or a realm raids a rival, every caster but the one nearest the seat marches with it (none if it has only one), and they walk home to the hall afterwards instead of vanishing (`WarParties.casters`, `BorderClashes`).
- **Town business on the talk screen** (Andy: "they all matter and propose screens for all of them, available through talk options"). **Town business** in MCA's menu, on any grown villager of a realm or village, opens a screen: call a festival (a lord or ruler), march with your realm's war party, mark a plot where you stand, choose what goes up on it (a second screen of the buildings), give it up, and have a courier carry what's in your hand (a second screen of the players about, by the names they go by). Each does what its command did, with the same rules and words (`TalkErrands`).
- **Raise a dead horse from its mane lock** (Andy: "add a screen at the wizards and necromancers that does it..."). **Raise my horse** shows on a court wizard or a necromancer while you hold a mane lock. A necromancer asks 24 emeralds' worth and raises it on the spot. A wizard warns you off; if you insist, they set you a task (bring them amethyst, phantom membrane, a ghast tear, echo shards or glow ink), and once it's done they teach you the rite: from then on, using a mane lock raises its horse yourself. The horse comes back with its name and breed but slower, shorter of wind and wilder, and once each blood moon it has a 35% chance to turn into a Nightmare or a Bat Horse (a donkey, mule or undead horse takes a horse's body for it) (`HorseRevival`).
- **Rival captains: hooks, pegs, illager marks and five more traits** (Andy: "please add them all"). A captain who comes back without an arm now wears a hook on a leather cuff where it was, and one without a leg an oak peg strapped on, posed with the limb that's gone. Illager captains show their face marks now (on their taller heads), and their hooks while their arms aren't folded. Five traits the plan named are their own now: **Senses ki** (feels a Z Fighter from 40 blocks, through walls, hidden or not, and comes for them), **Sworn to a god** (one of the seven, the same each time he comes back: his blows on another god's faithful land at 1.25 times, his own god's faithful's blows on him at 1.5), **Wears bog iron** (5 armour and no slower, but none while he's wet), **Never sleeps** (never caught abed, and keeps a watch all night that sees you sneaking or unseen), and **Hates fire mages** (goes for an Ember user, or whoever burned him, before anyone else, and hits them 1.5 times as hard).
- **The mask key takes school masks off and puts them on** (the Weapon Forms plan promised it). A worn school mask comes off into your pack (or drops if it's full); with one in your pack, the key puts it on, and whatever was on your head goes into the pack in its place. The hands go to the face as for every mask (Powers).
- **School bouts wait until you're ready, on a bracket screen, and taverns hold them too** (Andy: "there needs to be a screen so the fight starts when the user is ready, everytime these happen right now the user is caught off guard"). Entering a bout opens its bracket: you at the root and a branch to each fighter in the order you'll meet them, the rounds you've won ticked off and the next marked. No round starts until you press **Ready**; **Not yet** shuts it (it comes back in 30 s), and **Withdraw** leaves the bracket. Winning it all shows the bracket complete. And of an evening, a realm with a standing tavern has a bout master near it (once a day, smaller stakes: 3 silver for the final, 2 standing, 4 reputation, no golden apple). The menu line is now **Enter the school bout** (`Bouts`, `BoutBracketScreen`).
- **Cast Out wakes you with an empty map** (Andy: "fix it"). While your own kind are still turning you away (the first three days), Xaero's Minimap shows nothing, no waypoint is drawn in the world or on the map, and Xaero's World Map won't open ("You don't know these lands yet"). Nothing changes without Xaero (`CastOutMap`).
- **The chained road's figure is drawn in chains**: irons on both wrists and ankles and a chain from wrist to wrist across the front (`StoryChains`).
- **A ruler's boss bar keeps their name from strangers** (Andy: "fix it"). Until you've been introduced it reads "The King of Ardent" (their title and realm); once you have, their name as before.
- **Death messages name only those you know** (Andy: "yes it should say stranger, the only way you can find out after death is a new spell death sorcerers can cast called Speak with dead"). A player's death message now reads to each player as they knew the people in it: anyone you weren't introduced to is "a stranger", the dead and their killer alike (`DeadNames`).
- **Speak with Dead**, a new Death Sorcerer spell (scroll, tome page and staff, tier 2, 12 mana, Will): look at a body and the dead answer, giving who they were, how they died and who killed them (as they knew them), and you know their name from then on. Only a Death Sorcerer can cast it. Its scroll turns up where the Scroll of Whispers does.
- A mask now hides you everywhere. Every player is told who is masked, near or far. The tab list and the social interactions screen show a masked player with a cloth mask's face and by the name the tab list gives them, never their username. A villager no longer knows a masked parent, child or spouse: the family talk answers, the greeting as kin and what MCA's chat tells its AI all treat you as a stranger.
- An earthquake now traps people. One of a struck settlement's grown folk (two from magnitude 3) is shut in by fallen stone where they stand. A neighbour asks you to dig them out, and the trapped villager calls through the stones while anyone is near. Open a way out and go back to the neighbour for 8 coins' worth, hearts, reputation and standing. Left a day, their neighbours dig them out and the quest is taken back.
- Storm Rage: now and then a rust storm blows over Rustfall for about 3 minutes, one to two days apart, with a red-brown haze and a howling wind. While it blows, foes near you there are a world tier harder, and they go back to how they were when it passes. The only warning comes from someone who reads the sky. In the 5 minutes before it, a sky hermit or galactic patrolman near you tells you, wherever you are. With no one near, you get no warning, and there's never a bird.
- Cafe prices now follow brew grade and freshness. A brewed potion can go on a menu board as a tonic, priced by its grade: Crude 0.6x, Plain 1x, Fine 1.3x, Expert 1.7x, Masterwork 2.5x. Expert and Masterwork brews win a cafe the same reputation as a perfect dish. Kitchen's freshness clock counts too: fresh food sells at full price, food going stale at 0.75x, and food nearly off at 0.4x. Food that's nearly off also costs the cafe the reputation the serving would have won, and the customer says so.
- The Impaler (Inhabitants' model and animations, MIT) is a mob now: it comes up into deep caves only at night and lurks in the dark until it sees prey, runs it down fast and bites, throws the spikes off its arm at anything out of reach (they grow back slowly), and screams when hurt, bringing nearby stalactites down. By day one with nothing to hunt goes back into the rock; after a kill it leaves everything alone for a while. It has its own sounds now, and its coat follows its rock: dark slate in the dripstone caves, rust-brown under the badlands, tan elsewhere, and now and then an albino; their eyes glow. Dead, it drops bones and the spikes still on its arm.
- Impaler spikes concuss what they hit: you move slower, your view lags behind the mouse, sounds that start come muffled and your ears ring. A spike, a stick and a feather make four concussive arrows, which do the same from any bow.
- The spike drill (three spikes, copper and redstone; a Tool Station build in Powers) mines as an iron pickaxe, faster the longer it runs. It heats as it goes; too hot, it burns your hand and stalls until it cools. It cools at rest, and a snowball dropped on it in the inventory cools it at once.
- The Warped Clam (Inhabitants', MIT) lives on the End's outer islands, and so turns up in Arsenal's pocket rooms too. It never moves or hurts anyone; stand on it and it throws you high. It grows an ender pearl every day or two (faster beside chorus) and sits open with it showing; brush the pearl out and it starts another. A shovel eases it a block over, and anyone can lift and carry it.
- Powers' escort wards are walked by Powers, as its patrolmen are.
