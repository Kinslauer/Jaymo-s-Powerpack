# Frontier Armaments and Heraldry picks
- **Musketoon** (Andy: "call the other a musketoon and give it to the caravan guards, it should fire the cannonballs. long reload time, high damage"): a new hand gun, `jaymoskingdoms:musketoon`, that fires a cannonball. Hold use for 5 seconds to load it, then let go: it takes one WeaponMod cannonball from your pack (tag `jaymoskingdoms:musketoon_shot`), and nothing loads without one. Use it again to fire. The ball hits for 18 (a wall cannon's is 14), bursts over 2.5 blocks, kicks your aim up and shoves you back, and leaves a pit about three blocks across (mob griefing is always on). 200 shots to a gun. It counts as a gun for the Marksman.
- **Caravan guards**: every guard a realm or village hires for its carts, its trade caravans and its roaming merchants now carries one (their MCA guards too), with 3 to 5 balls each (a new kind in the finite ammo, restocked one at a time while calm). A guard fires at a foe in sight between 5 and 28 blocks, then takes 12 to 16 seconds to load, fighting with the butt meanwhile. His ball spares his own side, the villagers and golems he guards and his own realm's ground, as a wall gun's does. Greenskin and illager carts keep their own arms. A rich kingdom's musket issue leaves a caravan guard's musketoon alone.
- It has no recipe: a guard who is killed leaves his where he falls, and that is the only way to come by one.
- With Jaymo's Powers it is drawn animated in the hand (loaded, spent, reloading) and its shot gets the muzzle blast; without it, a plain 3D model. Model from cleannrooster's Frontier Armaments (MIT, `licenses/frontier-armaments-MIT.txt`).
- Built; not played.
- **Frontier armour** (ten sets from cleannrooster's Frontier Armaments, MIT; 62 pieces, drawn in 3D on anything humanoid). Each set says what it does in its tooltip.
  - **Templar plate** (Andy: "sold by the realms who's guards wear it, schematics as well, this should be for truth and sun followers"): the guards and archers of every kingdom and fiefdom whose patron is The Truth or the Crowned Sun wear it (their MCA guards too); a fiefdom in a Grim keep wears the black steel one. Those realms' armourers sell the four pieces and the Templar schematic, but only to a buyer who follows either faith. Hard to knock back.
  - **The Truth's sign is the Templar cross** (Andy: "the truth should get that templar cross sigil for everything"): its shrine glyph is the cross now (it was an open eye), and every realm that holds to The Truth bears a straight cross on its shields, surcoats and banners.
  - **Settler's gear** (Andy: "only the guards should wear the full helm"): village guards wear all of it, the kettle helm under the hat. Farmers and carters wear the hat alone with the coat and trousers, and the Family farmhand start gets the same. Village armourers sell the hat, coat, trousers, boots and the schematic. The helm is only forged.
  - **Ranger's harness**: musketeers and every caravan guard with a musketoon. Worn whole, guns load 15% quicker (WeaponMod's, the musketoon, and a guard's own loading). A kingdom with 150 in its treasury keeps the schematic at its armourer's.
  - **Hedge garb**: court wizards wear the wine-red one. Tailors sell the plain one; a kingdom's tailor sells the wine-red too, and the schematic for the ashen one.
  - **Primalist wrappings**: human raiders of camps in desert, badlands and savanna wear them and sometimes leave a piece. Worn whole, 10% faster on your feet. The horned dark version is the Primal Visage we already had.
  - **Juggernaut plate**: never made or sold. A barbarian chief leaves one piece when he falls, and a patron's champion leaves one from world tier Dread on. Very hard to knock back; every piece slows you 3%.
  - **Scoundrel's wrappings**: a speakeasy's fence and the smugglers wear them, and the fence sells them. A pickpocket has the hood up after dark and down by day. Worn whole, eating, drinking or drawing a bow doesn't slow your walk.
  - **Sanguine coat**: tailors sell it in red, blue or purple (each tailor stocks one colour). Worn whole, healing does 20% more.
  - **Despot's tusked plate** (Andy: "finish it for an orc warlord, and only they and green skins can wear it"): finished as a full set with our own tusked helm and greaves. Orc warchiefs wear it; a slain one always leaves the breastplate and each other piece one time in four. On a player who wasn't raised among goblins or orcs it slides off again. Worn whole it gives 8 points of absorption that grow back after 10 seconds unhurt.
  - **Foxshade mantle**: a bandit leader wears it and leaves it when killed; fletchers (the hunting lodge's hunters) wear it.
  - **Steel twins** (Andy: "these can be made from steel"): the black Templar and Ranger sets and the ashen Hedge garb are forged from steel at the Smithing Anvil (Jaymo's Powers), repaired with steel, with more toughness and knockback resistance.
- MCA guards kept losing issued gear to MCA's own dressing: a guard's musketoon now stays in his hands, and what a carter, fence or wizard was dressed in stays on.
- The new sets are filed as light or heavy armour, by armour class (padding, mail, plate) and for melting.
- Built; not played. How hats sit on MCA heads and hair is unchecked.
- **Heraldry fighting**: guards, barbarians and the other fighters who swing a weapon's own moves answer to its distance the way players do: get inside a spearman's reach and his blows lose force. Kingdoms' armour sets are filed as padding, mail or plate for Jaymo's Powers' cut, thrust and blunt rules.
