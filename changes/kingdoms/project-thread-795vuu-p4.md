# Blossom Lands, patches 4 on: kinds of village, and what follows
- **Villages thatch.** A Blossom village roofs every building in thatch until it has walled itself into a town (tier 3); from then on, and in the castle towns, new roofs are dark tile.
- **Rice paddies** (needs Kitchen): a bund of packed mud round a mud bed under standing water, planted with Kitchen's rice. One stands behind each village's shrine.
- **Three kinds of village, by the ground**: a temple village on high ground (40 or more above the sea: a second torii before its shrine and more lanterns), a market village on about three sites in ten lower down (a market square where the paddy would be), and the hamlet.
- **Farmsteads**: up to two outlying minka round each new village, 44 to 60 blocks from the bell where the ground will take one, each with its paddy (a plain field without Kitchen). Their people are the village's.
- **Graves**: a Blossom settlement's dead are buried under narrow andesite posts.
- Built; not seen in game (the dev server has no player to found a village for). Not built from this patch: stilt and snow villages (villages only rise in cherry groves so far) and the lighthouse.
- **Tea house**: a Blossom settlement's tavern is a tea house (tables on tatami, a hearth, tansu along the back, a brewer's corner with a cauldron and a brewing stand). It is the tavern to everything that uses one.
- **Dojo**: the Blossom land's training hall (tatami, shoji walls, training posts and racks); the school master stands in it as in any training hall.
- **Bath house** (walled Blossom towns, tier 3): a roofed stone pool. A soak in it counts as a bath.
- **Pagoda** (walled Blossom towns, tier 4): three storeys under skirts of roof, an altar on the ground floor.
- **Wayside shrines**: one by each farmstead, its altar given to one of the eight (or plain). The village shrine now holds an altar too.
- Not built from patch 5: a separate brewery (it is the tea house's back corner), roadside inns away from a village. None of these halls has been seen in game.
- **Blossom dress**: the people of a Blossom settlement (not its guards) wear a crossed robe and sash, eight outfits in all, the men's over dark trousers (`Uniforms`, `tools/gen_uniforms.py blossom`).
- **Straw Hat, Basket Hat, Straw Cape**: craftable from thatch and tatami, worn on the head and chest. The hats are drawn from their own models.
- **Ramie**, a fibre crop: a grown stand is cut by hand for 2 to 4 fibre and comes again from the root half grown; one cut in five leaves the root spent and it starts over. Breaking a grown stand gives seed. Three fibre make two string. A row of it grows by each farmstead's paddy, which is where seed comes from.
- Not built from patch 6: villagers wearing the hats, and a crest for each blossom house. The hats have not been seen on a head.
- **The levy** (needs MCA): one Blossom guard in three is a sworn man in the ronin harness, as all of them were; the other two are the levy, in straw hats with spears.
- **Watch towers**: each new village raises a timber watch tower 52 to 64 blocks out along the way to its torii, where the ground will take one. Nobody is posted in it yet.
- Not built from patch 7: matchlocks for the levy and a matchlock look for the musket, glaive guards at shrines, mounted road patrols.
- **The blossom smiths' steel.** Iron Sand comes up now and then (15%) with sand or gravel dug from under a river on Mohknee. A blast furnace burns it into a Steel Bloom. The bloom is folded on an anvil, six blows with it in hand, into the pack's steel ingot (an iron ingot without Powers).
- **It has to be taught.** A smith in a Blossom settlement has "Ask About Steel" on the Interact page and tells one step each time: the first for the asking, the second to someone holding iron sand, the third to someone holding a bloom. Until all three are heard the bloom will not fold for that player. Nothing on screen explains it otherwise.
- Not built from patch 8: new weapon heads (the Armory already has the glaive, claymore and flanged mace), a heirloom for each house, the shadow suit and the lord's armour. Not seen in game.
- **Blossom ruins**: about half of new Blossom villages have a fallen house 72 to 96 blocks out: walls down to the sill in places, the thatch gone in patches and gone to moss, no door, the hearth cold. Its chest was left behind and holds a little of what the house had (`chests/blossom_ruin`).
- Not built from patch 9: hired shadows sent by rival lords, the warlord's outlaw castle, and ruins as quest sites. The ruin has not been seen in game.
- **Maple and ginkgo** grow on Mohknee among the cherry (worldgen, kept to the far land), and a few are planted round each new Blossom village. Their leaves are green in spring and summer and turn red and gold in autumn, a few at a time (always turned without Kitchen's seasons). Both have saplings, dropped by the leaves.
- **Red spider lily**, in patches on Mohknee and planted beside each new grave in a Blossom settlement. It is poison in a stew. **Wisteria** hangs under leaves or any firm underside. **Bamboo thickets** on Mohknee.
- **Koi pond** (walled Blossom towns, tier 3): a garden pool under a wisteria arbour. **Koi** turn up in still water inside a Blossom settlement. They swim to anyone at the water holding food and to food thrown in, and eat from the hand. Each feed grows one a little and makes it stay for good; two grown, fed koi spawn a fry while the pond has room (8 blocks of water a fish, 10 at most). Unfed koi stay small and come and go.
- Not built from patch 10: villagers cutting the bamboo, pink moss fields, leaves falling in winter. Checked on the dev server: the trees, lilies and bamboo do generate on Mohknee. The pond, the koi's feeding and the turning leaves have not been seen in game.
- **Seven animals** among the cherry groves and on Mohknee, each made to the three rules (they are not bred by hand). All share one drawn-box model kind (`CreatureModel`), with a new "neck" movement for lowering the head.
  - **Snow Monkey**: troops. A hungry one eats a ripe crop down to the root by day. Fruit or roots from the hand feed the whole troop near for a day; a fed troop leaves the fields alone and screams when a monster comes within 12 blocks. In winter they sit in the still water of a Blossom settlement.
  - **Crane**: pairs. Picks over young crops by day, and each one it picks grows a stage. Pairs dance in spring. Runs from a sprinting player, not a walking one.
  - **Shrine Deer**: shy in the open, calm inside a Blossom settlement, where it comes to wheat, carrot, bread, apple or beetroot and bows for it. A hungry deer eats saplings; a fed one leaves them for a day.
  - **Raccoon Dog**: asleep by day and all winter. At night it eats food and bones left on the ground. After five meals it stops running from people.
  - **Serow**: alone, on one hillside (24 blocks) for life. Stands and stares at a player 7 to 18 blocks off and bolts if they come nearer. Grazes grass and fern short. No fall damage to speak of.
  - **Paper Hornet and its nest**: nests start by themselves under maple and ginkgo leaves on Mohknee. A nest keeps up to 3 hornets out by day; they hunt bees, silverfish and endermites. Shears cut **Paper Pulp** from a nest (up to 3, it grows back; one pulp crafts one paper). Cutting or breaking a nest with no lit campfire under it sets its hornets on you, and they poison.
  - **Giant Salamander**: in Mohknee's rivers, only in nobody's land with nothing built at the water. Lies on the bed; at night a hungry one takes a fish within 4 blocks, then nothing for a day. Left unhurt for a day, its stretch of river gets one cod or salmon a day.
- Not built from patches 11 and 12: monkeys raiding stalls, crane nests, boar rooting up fields, the hunting lodge's part. Checked on the dev server: every animal spawns by command and ticks without error. None has been seen in game, so shapes, poses and how they move are untested.
- **The dark of the moon** (the whole Overworld): on an ordinary night, natural monster spawns under the open sky follow the moon. At the full moon none appear; at the gibbous half stay away; at the quarters a fifth. At the new moon half of them bring a second of their kind, at the thin crescents a fifth. Blood, harvest and the pack's other moons keep their own rules; caves and roofed places are unchanged.
- **Four night things** of the blossom lands, under our own names. Nothing on screen explains them; Blossom villagers drop hints in gossip (ten lines).
  - **River Child**: spawns at night in river and cherry grove water on blossom land. Goes for players who are in the water and pulls them down; leaves people on the bank alone. Takes a ripe crop from a field by the water at night. Food dropped near it is taken as a gift: the thrower is left alone for 3 days.
  - **Snow Widow** (great foe, boss bar, posture): comes to a player on snowed-over cherry land or Mohknee on a winter night (needs Kitchen's seasons). Freezes everyone within 7 blocks, slows within 4, takes double harm from fire, gone at first light. 120 health.
  - **The Unburied** (great foe, boss bar, posture): at night, where 8 or more corpses of people or monsters lie within 16 blocks of each other near a player (needs Kitchen's corpses), they rise as one giant skeleton and the bodies are gone. 220 health, heavy knockback. Burying or burning the dead prevents it.
  - **Black Swell**: at night in a sea fog bank on the Mohknee crossing or round its coast, it rises 30 blocks ahead of a sailor and closes on the nearest ship or boat. Alongside, it floods a ship's hold each second and breaks small boats. It cannot be hurt. A barrel (or tansu) thrown into the water near it sends it down. It also leaves at dawn or after two minutes.
- Not built from patches 13 and 14: the mountain beasts for the hunting lodge, the crow-winged hermit and his Weapon Form, festivals moved to full moons. Checked on the dev server: all four spawn by command and tick without error. None has been seen in game; how they rise for a real player is untested.
- **The pale grove** (patch 15). A new Nether biome, `jaymoskingdoms:pale_grove`, beside the others (nothing replaced; only new Nether chunks get it). Wide patches of pale moss over the netherrack, pale oaks on the moss, and **Pale Wisteria** (new block, light 6, our own texture) hanging under their leaves and in strings up to 18 long from the cave roofs. Pale fog and white ash in the air. Fire ogres are far more common there than in the rest of the Nether; skeletons, a few endermen and striders too. `/locate biome jaymoskingdoms:pale_grove` finds one.
- Not built from patch 15: the slow river and the ogre keep. Checked on the dev server: the biome generates (a 128 by 128 area held 1,270 wisteria, 615 logs, 2,534 moss). Not seen in game, so the fog colour and the look are untested.
- **The sunken palace** (patch 16). Three stand in the deep sea off Mohknee, one on each of three headings from the land's middle, at the first place past the shore where the water is 24 deep (the same places every time for a given world seed). One is built on the seabed the first time a player comes within 96 blocks: a 25 by 17 platform, a pillared veranda round a walled hall, a dais with a gold block, a wide roof with turned-up corners, a small upper hall and a stepped second roof, all prismarine, lit by sea lanterns and flooded. 8 guardians keep it and do not despawn. 4 chests (`chests/sunken_palace`: gold, prismarine, emeralds, nautilus shells, a few diamonds, a rare trident, 1 chance in 4 of a heart of the sea each). Nothing marks it from the surface; two new lines of blossom gossip hint at it. Operators: `/kingdom mohknee palace` says where the nearest one is, `/kingdom mohknee palace build` builds one on the seabed where you are.
- Not built from patch 16: sea folk (Tideborn) as its keepers; guardians keep it for now. Checked on the dev server: the world had 3 sites, one was built at depth 31 with no air pockets, its chests carry the loot table and 8 guardians were there. Not seen in game, so its look is untested.
