# Medieval picks, patch 7: Sieges and war

- A war party at a seat lays siege in five waves, each about 75 seconds, with a 40-second lull between. In a lull the attackers fall back to a camp behind their banner and their engines go quiet. The defenders are bound up, their archers get more arrows and the worst breaches are mended out of the treasury. Each wave after the first brings two fresh soldiers from the attackers' home. Hold out through all five and the siege is broken (the defenders thank you); fall and the seat is sacked as before. A horn sounds as each wave starts and ends. (`Sieges`, `WarParties`, `SiegeTrain`, `Haul`)
- When a siege begins, the seat's ruler, or else a guard, walks up to each unsworn player nearby and asks on the talk card which side they're on. Defend (its guards treat you as a friend and the war chest is open to you), join the attackers (you march and share their spoils, and the seat's guards come for you), or stay out (the attackers leave you be). A sworn player is on their realm's side without being asked. If you never answer, deliberately hitting one side's guards puts you on the other side, and hitting the seat's guards costs the same standing as joining the attackers. Hitting the seat's guards after saying you'll stay out counts as assault. (`Sieges`)
- Sappers: two of a party's guards carry a log up to the keep's door and batter it in (the cracks show as they swing), or with no keep the nearest wooden door (at a homestead, only the homestead's own doors, which drop when broken). Its keg man carries real powder kegs, two at most, bought from his realm's treasury at the muster. He digs a hole under the wall beside the door (a few seconds per block), sets a keg and lights it, then runs until it blows; his second keg goes on the other side of the door. The kegs he carries are the only blasts there are. Any he hasn't used go back to the treasury, even after a restart, and sappers do nothing with mob griefing off. Sappers get their shields back when the siege ends. (`Sappers`)
- The war chest: when the siege begins the keep's door is shut with a timber barricade. Talk to a besieged seat's ruler while you're sworn to it, rule it, or chose to defend it, and the card spends its treasury: two more guards (paid off afterwards), masons to mend the walls, arrows for every archer, an iron gate in place of the timber, and a fighting draught (Strength and Resistance for two minutes). What shots and kegs broke is remembered, so it can be put back. Every purchase is checked again when you pick it. The barricade comes down when the siege is over. The barricade and breaches are saved with the world: after a restart, or if their land wasn't loaded when the siege ended, the barricade comes down and the breaches are mended the next time that land loads. (`WarChest`, `SiegeScars`)
- A lord who's been threatened spends on his defences. After a siege on his seat or a raid on his town, if the treasury can spare it, he orders a gatehouse, a tower or barracks (whichever he has fewest of) for his builders, and says so. At most every two days. (`Defences`)
- Monster feuds in the wild: zombies and skeletons go for illagers and illagers for them, wither skeletons for piglins, and orcs and goblins for each other when both are away from their camps. Never on a realm's land or in a raid. (`Feuds`, `Orcs`, `Goblins`)
- Hunting parties: a realm or camp that hates you may send hunters after you, more of them the better your armour and weapon (one to six). Never near a town or on a realm's land, at most once every 30 minutes (saved on you), and they give up after five minutes or once they lose you. (`Hunts`)
- Raids on your home: a realm at war with yours may march on your homestead, engines and sappers too, while you're near it. They lay siege in one wave; if they're still standing at the end, they empty what's stored there into their cart. At most every 40 minutes, saved on you. (`HomeRaids`, `WarParties`)
- The white flag: raise a plain white banner (use it in the air, in your main hand; hold right-click to keep it up) and the guards or bandits fighting you stop, if your standing with them isn't too low. You get one truce per fight: a side that has given you a truce won't honour your flag again for ten minutes after it ends. Strike them under the flag and they come back at you and won't honour it for twenty minutes. A band's tongue-speaker comes to name a toll (`Parleys`). Go up to a realm's guard and you can pay what you owe (the whole fine or nothing, once a day: the hunt for you is called off and your standing improves a little), give yourself up (`Busts`), or leave in peace. These waits are saved on you. (`WhiteFlags`)
- Enemy colours: wear a surcoat or tabard in a realm's colour and carry its arms on your shield or banner (or on the surcoat itself), and its guards take you for one of their own at a distance. Come close and one looks you over; stay there and he sees through you. Speak near them, talk to one of their people, or hit any of their people and you're found out at once. Guards look from further off and see through you faster while their realm is at war. (`Colours`)
- A speakeasy fixer stands by the bookie. For a price he clears your name with a realm that thinks ill of you: up to 150 points of ill will, and their hunt for you is called off. Once a day per realm, saved on you. (`Fixers`, `Underworld`)
- Court casters march with their realm's war party and stay with it at the siege. They cast at the enemy realm's guards and archers as well as its folk, and at players on the other side of a siege. (`Workers`, `WarParties`, `CourtWizards`)
