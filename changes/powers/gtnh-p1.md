# GT New Horizons picks, patch 1: Stone Age recipes
- **Gravel gives no flint**: breaking gravel only ever drops gravel. Flint comes from loose flint on the ground (as before), the pan (as before), or the **sieve**: a plant mesh used on a gravel block sifts it away and leaves a flint 30% of the time; one sift in 8 wears the mesh through.
- **Flint in the first table and chest**: crafting table = 3 logs + 1 flint in the 2x2 grid (four logs and a flint don't fit in the starting grid); chest = 4 logs, 4 planks and a flint round them. Vanilla's plank recipes are off.
- **Doors and fences cost more**: every wooden door is 6 of its planks and a copper or iron nugget for 1 door (`#jaymospowers:door_fittings`); the iron door takes a steel ingot (6 iron + 1 steel for 3). Fences and gates take **fence rails** where they took sticks. Cobblestone walls unchanged.
- **The saw does more** (`layers/SawItem`, stays in the grid worn): saw + 2 planks = 8 sticks (by hand still 2 a plank), saw + 3 planks of a wood = 8 of its slabs, saw + a wooden slab = 2 fence rails.
- **Paper is pressed**: sugar cane alone no longer makes paper. Three pulp (**sawdust**, sticks ground in the mortar, or **cane chad**, sugar cane ground in the mortar) and a bucket of water (or a clay bucket of water) pressed between two stone slabs in the grid make 3 paper; the slabs come back. The plant-mesh pulp, Kitchen's bark and Kingdoms' pulp routes are unchanged.
- **Torches by fuel**: a coal or charcoal and a stick make 2 torches (were 4). Coal coke (5) and creosote rags (3) join in patch 4 (`tools/gen_stone_age.py`, `TORCH_YIELDS`). Kingdoms' burning-down torches are untouched.
- **The mortar grinds more** (`alchemy/brewing/MortarEntity`): flint into flint dust, clay balls into clay dust, iron, copper and gold ingots into their dust (the Xeno-Band's dusts), sugar cane into cane chad, sticks into sawdust, one for one, in the same grinding game. Metal dust melts in the smeltery or melter twice as fast as the ingot (`Melting.DUST_SPEEDUP`). Three copper dust and a tin dust (`#c:dusts/tin`, empty until tin comes in patch 4) mix into 4 **bronze dust** in the grid.
- **Bricks need a form**: clay balls no longer smelt or fire into bricks. A **brick form** (a plank carved with a knife; the knife comes back worn) pressed with a clay block or four clay balls gives 4 **unfired bricks**, and the brick kiln fires those into bricks. The form wears out after 32 pressings.
- **Clay bucket**: 3 clay balls or clay dust shaped like a bucket make an **unfired clay bucket**, which the kiln fires. It scoops and pours water, milks cows and goats (and the milk is drunk from it), works as a water bucket in the paper press, and is used up a little each time it's emptied (a durability bar; 20 uses) until it cracks. Dipped in lava it shatters.
- **Glass is cast**: sand no longer smelts into glass. 4 sand and a flint dust make 4 **glass dust**, which melts in the smeltery into **molten glass** (1000 mB each); the casting basin casts it into glass blocks, and a **pane cast** (gold poured over a glass pane) casts 250 mB into a glass pane. Glass and panes melt back down. Villager glass trades kept.
- **No self-mending early**: Tinkering's Overslime and the Mending enchantment do nothing until the holder has Kingdoms' Knowledge level 3 (the Skeleton Lord slain); without Kingdoms they work as before. A refused Mending's XP goes to you as plain XP.
- **Wood and flint tools can't be mended**: a Tinkering tool with a wood or flint head can't be repaired at the Tool Station, and no wood or flint tool (`#jaymospowers:unmendable`: vanilla's wooden tools, Armory's wooden weapons, the hand axe, primitive knife and spear, throwing stick) mends at the anvil or by putting two together in the grid.
- **JEI**: every new grid recipe on the crafting page; a **Sifting** page (the sieve and the pan's finds, with their chances), a **Mortar Grinding** page, the kiln page's unfired bricks and clay bucket, Tinkering's melting (dusts, glass), basin (glass) and casting (pane cast, panes) pages; info pages for the clay bucket and brick form.
